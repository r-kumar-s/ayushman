BlogIBS & Constipation

Constipation can involve infrequent bowel movements, hard or dry stools, straining or a feeling of incomplete evacuation. Occasional constipation is common, but persistent symptoms should be assessed appropriately.

Ayurveda considers bowel habits as part of digestive health and may look at food, hydration, daily routine, activity, sleep and individual constitution when planning supportive care.

What Can Contribute to Constipation?

Low fluid intake, insufficient dietary fibre, reduced physical activity, changes in routine and some medicines can contribute to constipation. Individual triggers can vary.

Ayurvedic Perspective on Bowel Regularity

Traditional Ayurvedic assessment may consider the quality and regularity of digestion and elimination together with the person's broader lifestyle. The aim is to understand the individual pattern rather than treating constipation in isolation.

Helpful Daily Habits

  • Drink adequate fluids according to your individual needs.
  • Include fibre-containing foods as appropriate for your tolerance.
  • Maintain regular meal and sleep routines.
  • Stay physically active within your capabilities.
  • Allow adequate time for bowel movements and avoid repeatedly ignoring the urge.

When to Seek Medical Evaluation

Seek medical advice for persistent constipation, blood in the stool, unexplained weight loss, severe abdominal pain, vomiting, fever, marked abdominal swelling or a new significant change in bowel habits.

Personalized Ayurvedic Care

An Ayurvedic practitioner may review digestion, bowel habits, diet, routine and health history before recommending an individualized plan. Traditional therapies should be used under qualified professional guidance when indicated.
